Where the app's data come from, and who we thank.

Product data

Open Pet Food Facts

Part of the products come from Open Pet Food Facts, the free, collaborative database of pet food, maintained by volunteers and the Open Food Facts association.

Tutora contains information from Open Pet Food Facts, which is made available under the Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0. Individual contents of the database are under the Database Contents License (DbCL) 1.0. Product photos are under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike (CC BY-SA) 3.0 licence and may show third-party trademarks.

In the app, products from Open Pet Food Facts show "Dati: Open Pet Food Facts, licenza ODbL" and, for photos, "Foto: Open Pet Food Facts, licenza CC BY-SA", with a link to the product page. Open Pet Food Facts data are written by volunteers: we mark them as "to be verified" until they are confirmed. You can also correct them directly on Open Pet Food Facts.

For products we do not have, our server also queries the public API of Open Food Facts, under the same terms.

User photos

Many products were added by people using the app, with photos of the labels. Thank you.

Online shops and brand websites

For some products we read the mandatory label texts (composition, analytical constituents, additives) from public pages of online shops and brand websites. We rewrite them in our format. We do not use descriptions, photos or logos from these pages. In the app these products are marked as provisional, with the source shown, and are replaced when a photo of the label arrives.

References for the score

  • EU Register of Feed Additives, European Commission, Food and Feed Information Portal. Reuse allowed with acknowledgement of the source (Decision 2011/833/EU). © European Union.
  • European Union law from EUR-Lex: regulations on additives, feed labelling, the catalogue of feed materials and dietetic feed. © European Union.
  • FEDIAF (European Pet Food Industry Federation): Nutritional Guidelines 2025 and Code of Good Labelling Practice 2019, for nutritional reference values and ingredient categories.
  • Open Food Facts ingredient taxonomy: Italian names and synonyms of ingredients. We treat it as ODbL data.
  • Scientific opinions and studies cited in the risk table: EFSA, IARC, FDA and veterinary journal articles. Each source is shown in the app next to the explanation and on the Scoring method page.

Recalls

Recall notices come from official sources. Every recall in the app links the original notice, which always prevails.

  • Italian Ministry of Health, food product notices and recalls: the main source for Italy.
  • Recall pages of supermarket chains and pet shops selling in Italy.
  • Official foreign sources: the European Commission's RASFF alert system, the UK Food Standards Agency and the Bavarian food safety authority (LGL). We use them only for products known to be sold in Italy, and the alert says in which country the recall is official.

News from newspapers and specialised websites is used only as a hint to look for the official notice: we never publish a recall without an official source.
