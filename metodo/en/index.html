Scoring method

Method version: v3.3 · Last updated: 4 November 2026 · Translation: the Italian version prevails.

The score goes from 0 to 100. We calculate it with fixed rules, the same for every product, only from what the label declares. Every point removed or added appears in the app with an explanation and, for substances, the source.

The score is indicative information. It is not veterinary advice and does not say whether a product suits your pet. For diets, allergies and diseases ask your veterinarian.

We judge substances and label information, never brands. Nobody pays for scores.

1. Parts of the score

PartPointsWhat it measures
Nutrition30Protein, carbohydrates and fat on dry matter, different for dogs and cats
Ingredients40How clear and how good the ingredient list is
Additives20Additives with a documented risk, colourants, additives declared without a name
Organic10EU organic logo

Each part starts at its maximum and goes down with the rules below, never below zero. Each fact counts once.

2. Bands

Band (as shown in the app)Score
Eccellente (excellent)75 to 100
Buono (good)50 to 74
Scarso (poor)25 to 49
Cattivo (bad)0 to 24

Two final caps:

  • if nutritional values are missing, or the first ingredient is not recognised, the score is partial and stops at 74 (good);
  • if the product contains a red level ingredient or additive, the score stops at 49 (poor).

If a product is for both dogs and cats, we score both and show the lower result. In packs with several flavours we score every flavour and the pack gets the score of the worst one.

3. Nutrition (30 points)

We compare values on dry matter, that is without water, so dry and wet foods can be compared: value on dry matter = value ÷ (100 − moisture) × 100.

Carbohydrates are not on the label: we estimate them by difference (100 − protein − fat − fibre − ash − moisture).

Protein is worth 15 points, carbohydrates 10, fat 5. For protein and carbohydrates: full level 100% of the points, partial 60%, low 30%, below threshold 0.

Source: FEDIAF, Nutritional Guidelines for Complete and Complementary Pet Food for Cats and Dogs, 2025 edition, tables III-3a (dog) and III-4a (cat). FEDIAF has no values for senior animals: for them we use adult values. If the label says "all life stages" we use growth values.

Moisture not declared. For a dry food we assume 10% and say so in the app. The law requires moisture to be declared when above 14% (Reg. (EC) 767/2009, Annex I), so a dry food without a declared value is below that.

Without nutritional values. For treats and complementary foods nutrition is not scored: the other parts (70 points) are scaled to 100 and the app says "Valutazione senza dati nutrizionali". For a complete food without values the app says "Voto parziale" and asks for a photo of the back. In both cases the score stops at 74.

  • If, inside a generic category, the label names species with percentages adding up to at least 26% ("meat and animal derivatives (chicken 20%, beef 10%)"), the generic penalty is halved.
  • "Equal to 100% of the meat used" means all the meat is of that species, not that the product is 100% that meat.
  • If we do not recognise the first ingredient we remove no points, but the score becomes partial (at most 74) and the product goes to review.
  • Generic categories follow the FEDIAF labelling code (2019), Annex 1, based on Directive 82/475/EEC.

5. Additives (20 points)

Each additive is linked to its code in the EU Register of Feed Additives (Reg. (EC) 1831/2003), by name and by code. We do not use approximate matching: if a name does not match, the additive is not guessed.

RulePoints
Substance in the risk table: yellow / orange / red level−6 / −15 / −30
Any colourant, named or not: colour is of no use to the animal−6
Category declared without a name ("antioxidants", "preservatives", "gelling agents")−4 each, at most −12

The same substance counts once, at its highest level. An unnamed "colourants" counts once, as a colourant.

The limit of European labels

European rules allow some additives to be declared only by their category, for example "antioxidants" or "preservatives", without saying which substance it is. This is legal. But in these cases we cannot know which additive is used: that is why we remove 4 points for the lack of transparency, not for a risk. In the same way, a label that declares no additives gets all 20 points, but this does not prove there are none.

Texts always use words such as "risk" or "possible". A substance authorised in the EU remains legal: the level only means that a documented risk or a scientific doubt exists.

6. Organic (10 points)

10 points if the package has the EU organic logo, otherwise 0.

7. Products without a score

  • Veterinary dietetic foods (feed for particular nutritional purposes, Reg. (EU) 2020/354): no score, because they are to be used on a veterinarian's advice. The app shows composition, additives and values.
  • Species not stated: the app asks whether it is for dogs or cats. The score is valid at once for you; it becomes the product score for everyone when at least two different users give the same answer and nobody gives the opposite one.

When the score does not appear

The score appears only if we recognise at least 60% of the ingredients on the label. Below this threshold we do not show it, because it would rest on too much uncertain data: we ask you for a photo of the label. This is not the same as a partial score, which applies when the first ingredient or the nutritional values are missing, and in that case the score does not exceed 74.

8. Where the data come from

A product's label may come, in order of priority, from photos sent by users or by us, from Open Pet Food Facts, from online shop pages or from brand websites. The app always shows the source. Data not yet checked are marked as "to be verified" or "provisional". Readings made with artificial intelligence go through automatic checks and, if in doubt, a review by a person. More on the Sources and credits page.

9. What the score does not know

  • Additive quantities are not on the label: a substance counts the same at 1 mg/kg and at the legal maximum.
  • Carbohydrates are estimated, and for a dry food without declared moisture we assume 10%.
  • Contaminants, raw material quality and digestibility are not on the label and are not assessed.
  • Additives declared only by category cannot be identified (section 5).
  • Data entered by volunteers may contain transcription errors.
  • The individual animal: age, health and special needs change everything. That is why you need a veterinarian.

11. Method updates

When we change the rules we publish a new version and recalculate all scores. Previous versions stay archived. If you think a score is wrong, write to support.tutora@gmail.com with the barcode: we will check it again. Manufacturers can write to the same address to report an updated label.
